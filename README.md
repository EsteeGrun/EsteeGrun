# Hi, I'm Katya Gruen

I am a Ph.D. student in **Artificial Intelligence** with a background in **mathematics, statistics, machine learning, and data science**. My research interests focus on **continual learning, adaptive memory, LLM-based systems, cybersecurity AI, concept drift, and robust sequential decision-making**.

My current research direction is centered on:

> **Adaptive Threat Memory: drift-aware continual learning for LLM-based cybersecurity systems.**

I am especially interested in how AI systems can adapt to new threats, new tasks, and changing data distributions without catastrophically forgetting what they previously learned.

---

## Research Focus

### Adaptive Threat Memory

I study continual-learning methods for cybersecurity systems that must adapt to new attack patterns while preserving older threat knowledge.

Core questions:

* How can a threat detector learn new attacks without forgetting old ones?
* How can replay memory reduce catastrophic forgetting?
* How can models detect and respond to concept drift?
* How can LLM-based systems explain alerts without hallucinating unsupported reasons?

Current focus areas:

* Continual learning for intrusion detection.
* Catastrophic forgetting under attack-family streams.
* Replay buffers and memory selection.
* Drift-aware threat modeling.
* False-positive analysis.
* Local alert explanations.
* LLM-assisted cybersecurity reasoning.

---

### Continual Learning in LLM-Based Systems

I am also building benchmark pipelines for continual learning in intent classification and LLM-based decision systems.

Current benchmark datasets include:

* CLINC150
* BANKING77
* HWU64

The goal is to test whether models can learn sequential groups of intents while preserving performance on previous tasks.

Methods of interest:

* Naive sequential fine-tuning.
* Random replay.
* Uncertainty-based replay.
* Similarity-based replay.
* Drift-aware replay.
* Elastic Weight Consolidation.
* Adapter and LoRA-style fine-tuning.
* Memory routing across fast, medium, and slow adaptation modules.

---

### Risk-Aware Sequential Decision-Making

I also explore risk-aware AI systems in quantitative finance as a secondary research domain.

This work studies volatility-conditioned routing, adverse-selection risk, and market microstructure as examples of sequential decision-making under regime shift.

I treat this work as a research prototype, not as a live-trading system. The main purpose is to study how AI policies can become more conservative under high-risk regimes while preserving useful long-term memory.

Topics include:

* GARCH volatility modeling.
* Regime-conditioned policy routing.
* Risk overlays.
* Walk-forward evaluation.
* Microstructure simulation.
* Statistical robustness.
* Drawdown and tail-risk control.
